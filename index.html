Computational mechanics

Numerical simulation of fluids, shock waves, blast and impact.

Rivers Research computes how fluids and structures behave under conditions that are difficult or costly to test. Each method is first checked against a published experiment, and the agreement is reported with the result.

Send an enquiry Validation cases

Background: three-dimensional dam break impacting an obstacle; MARIN experiment, SPHERIC Test 2.

Scope

Problem classes

Turbulence

Turbulent flow resolved directly, including the transport and mixing of particles and scalars.

Blast and detonation

Blast loading in the open, in enclosed rooms and between buildings, and the structure of detonation fronts.

Shock and multiphase flow

Shock waves interacting with gas and liquid interfaces, including bubble collapse near walls.

Free-surface flow, impact and porous media

Wave impact on structures, deformation of metals at high rates, and flow through porous materials.

Cases

Validation cases

Eleven cases, each compared with a published experiment or reference solution. The figure linked from each case gives the full comparison. All were computed on a 192-core workstation.

Dam break against an obstacle

A column of water is released in a tank and strikes a box. The computed forces on the box are compared with the MARIN laboratory measurements.

Mean loads on the box within −2.7 to +6.4 % of the measured MARIN data · impact peak +10 %

Validation figure

Isotropic turbulence

The fine vortex structure of a turbulent flow, resolved directly on a 512³ grid without a turbulence model.

512³ DNS, Re_λ ≈ 240 · Kolmogorov constant 1.72 · derivative skewness −0.55 · k_max·η = 1.17

Validation figure

Particle dispersion and mixing in turbulence

Tracer particles are followed through a turbulent flow to measure how quickly they spread, and the result is compared with Taylor's classical theory.

Tracer dispersion matches Taylor's ballistic limit to 0.2 % · Lagrangian/Eulerian variance 0.991

Validation figure

Cellular detonation in hydrogen, oxygen and argon

A detonation front develops the diamond-shaped cell pattern that soot foils record in laboratory experiments.

Front speed +0.11 % from Chapman–Jouguet · cell width 20 mm vs 23.7–27.3 mm measured (Strehlow 1967)

Validation figure

Explosion in a closed room

Shock waves from a charge reflect between the walls of a room. The loads on the walls and the final gas pressure are computed.

Incident shock within ±6 % of Kingery–Bulmash at Z ≥ 1 · mass conserved exactly, energy to 3×10⁻⁴

Validation figure

Surface burst of a 2 kg charge

The blast wave from a charge on the ground, compared with the standard Kingery–Bulmash blast curves.

Peak overpressure −5 to −8 % from Kingery–Bulmash beyond Z = 1.5 · arrival time within 1–11 %

Validation figure

Shock wave passing a helium cylinder

A shock wave deforms a column of helium into a pair of vortices, as in the Haas & Sturtevant experiment.

All 6 wave and interface speeds within the ±11 % uncertainty of Haas & Sturtevant (1987)

Validation figure

Collapse of an air bubble near a wall

A shock wave in water collapses an air bubble beside a rigid wall, a mechanism of cavitation damage.

Collapse time within 0.5 % of Rayleigh, grid-converged · peak wall pressure not converged (stated)

Validation figure

Taylor impact of a copper rod

A copper rod strikes a rigid anvil at 190 m/s and deforms plastically. The final shape is compared with published results.

Final length within 0.4–0.9 % of Johnson & Cook's computation · energy balance closes to 0.07 %

Validation figure

Flow through a porous medium

Fluid moves between randomly packed grains, and the permeability of the material is computed from the resolved flow.

Square-array permeability within 0.004–0.15 % of Barnett et al. (2016) · second-order convergence

Validation figure

Blast in a city street intersection

A 100 kg charge at street level. The loads on the building facades are computed, with the shielding and channelling caused by the streets.

Free field → Kingery–Bulmash with mesh refinement (within 4 % at 5 cm) · facade impulse within 5–7 % of DDESB TP-20

Procedure

Validation and reporting procedure

Reference case

A method is first applied to a problem with a known answer from the literature.

Grid convergence

Each case is computed at more than one resolution, and quantities that have not converged are identified.

Reported deviation

Every result is stated with its difference from the reference, including where the agreement is weaker.
