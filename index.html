Bible

AndroidiOS

The King James Version and the World English Bible, with the Hebrew and Greek underneath every word. Tap an underlined word to see the original, its grammar in plain English, a short meaning, the pronunciation and Strong's dictionary entry. From there, list every verse where the same Hebrew or Greek word stands, counted from the original text rather than from a translation's tags.

  • Hebrew and Greek text of every verse, word by word, with transliteration and gloss
  • Highlights in four colours, notes, bookmarks, and marked passages that run over several verses
  • Search the text or the dictionary, or type a reference such as John 3:16 or a Strong's number such as G26
  • Poetry keeps its line breaks; footnotes and cross references open with a tap
  • Light, sepia and dark themes; text size in eight steps, or pinch to resize
  • Works without a connection; the whole text and dictionary ship inside the app

LiveKey

Android

For the keyboard player who has to join a song that has already started. Tap Listen, point the phone at the singing, and it reports the key to play in, the chord sounding now, the tempo and the time signature. The key usually settles within two or three seconds.

  • Key and mode, the relative key, the seven diatonic chords, and how sharp or flat the singers are of A = 440 Hz
  • The chords heard since the song began, with roman numerals or Nashville numbers
  • Hold freezes the key under your hands; Transpose shifts everything to what you should play
  • Beats per minute with a pulsing beat counter that marks the downbeat
  • Presets for a band, a guitar, or voices alone
  • All signal processing runs on the phone; no audio leaves the device
Android 8.0 and up
Research

Fluid dynamics, with code that tests itself first.

Two lines of work. In both, the solver is checked against known results before it is used to say anything new.

When does a growing boundary layer turn over?

Lay a heavier fluid over a lighter one, or heat a surface suddenly, and the unstable layer thickens with time. Because the base state keeps changing, stability cannot be read from a single eigenvalue problem. This work solves the linear initial-value problem with the operator re-evaluated at every step, derives the exact discrete adjoint of the time-stepper, and finds the initial disturbance that grows most, following the modified optimisation of Daniel, Tilton and Riaz (2013).

It covers porous media first and then the full Navier–Stokes equations at finite Prandtl number, with a two-dimensional direct simulation to check that the linear optimum survives at finite amplitude. One application is immersion cooling: the dimensionless onset is mapped to seconds and millimetres for dielectric coolants such as FC-72 and FC-40.

Is the bell in the conditional dissipation real?

When the dissipation rate of turbulence is averaged conditionally on a passive scalar, a bell-shaped profile appears, large enough to matter for mixing models. Earlier work resolved it with two bins, and two points cannot tell a shape. A parallel pseudospectral code for incompressible isotropic turbulence with one passive scalar was written to find out, in Fortran 2008 with MPI, FFTW and parallel HDF5.

The conditional-averaging routines are a separate, unit-tested subsystem that can be pointed at data from any code. Every output carries both fixed-width and equal-population bins over the same samples, so the two can be compared directly. So far the bell survives equal-population binning, and a large part of its amplitude depends on the scalar being bounded.

About

An independent software and research company.

Rivers Research LLC builds and publishes the apps on this page and carries out the research described above. The apps are written in TypeScript with React Native and in Kotlin; the research code in Python and Fortran.

The Bible texts, dictionaries and fonts inside the apps come from public-domain and open-licence sources. Each app lists them, with every change that was made, under Sources and licences in its settings.

On the deviceApps carry their data with them and run without a connection. Notes and highlights are stored on the phone and travel with its normal backup.
Nothing collectedNo accounts, no analytics, no advertising. The privacy policy fits on one page.
Checked before claimedResearch code is validated against analytic solutions and published thresholds before it is pointed at an open question.
